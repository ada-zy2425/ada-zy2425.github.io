Research in progress·Two-view geometry

Extreme-View Geometry Recovery (EVGR)

Source-conditioned routing over complementary relative-pose hypotheses

EVGR estimates the relative pose between two calibrated images. Dense matching, depth-assisted solving and direct camera regression each fail on different pairs, so EVGR keeps all three as complete pose hypotheses. A small learned router then predicts each candidate's rotation and translation error from geometry alone and returns one unchanged pose.

  • Three complete sources. RoMa-Ω matches feed calibrated PoseLib and, with monocular depth, MADPose; a camera regressor gives a third, matching-free pose.
  • Route, don't average. Features are Sampson residuals on the matching support, cross-candidate rotation/translation distances, and coverage statistics.
  • Dominance-gated switch. The matching pose stays the default; the router leaves it only when a candidate is predicted better on both error axes.

Research in progress·Multi-view hand reconstruction

Form2Frame

Calibrated multi-view hand meshes from frozen monocular priors

Given synchronized RGB crops and known cameras, Form2Frame returns a 778-vertex MANO-topology mesh in the reference camera's metric frame. HaMeR and WiLoR stay frozen. Only a small cross-view module and a regional scorer are trained.

  • Support follows visibility. Per-joint view weights combine the reprojection residual with a learned support term, so occluded views lose weight before triangulation.
  • One metric frame. Every monocular mesh is placed onto the triangulated scaffold by robust Sim(3) (IRLS, Huber loss), forming a reliability-weighted anchor mesh.
  • Each region picks its source. Six anatomical regions allocate weight across up to 16 HaMeR/WiLoR candidates using geometric agreement and paired image evidence.

CondDepth

Camera-space hand reconstruction from a single RGB image and known intrinsics

CondDepth keeps WiLoR's root-relative hand shape and replaces only its absolute placement. Trained readouts predict where the wrist sits along its camera ray, using the frozen visual token, the predicted geometry and the camera intrinsics.

  • Scale is a gauge, not a size. WiLoR's native scale compensates for pose and projection errors. Inserting anatomically correct scale makes placement worse, so the root is corrected instead.
  • Placement along the ray. Query decoders cross-attend to spatial tokens and camera rays; radial heads then refine depth along each ray before a fixed-weight ensemble.
  • Whole population kept. Missed detections fall back to a camera-conditioned prior or a bounded full-image rescue, and they stay in the error mean.

Robust Sparse 3DGS

Sparse-view reconstruction, numerical stability and camera-error repair

A COLMAP / VGGT → gsplat workflow for sparse-view training, held-out rendering, PLY export and a local WebGL viewer. The engineering focus is failure analysis: every fix is tested against a frozen contract on matched experiment arms.

  • Initialization guard. Duplicate points produced zero nearest-neighbour scales and log(0); only the degenerate scales are clamped.
  • Camera error isolated. A controlled 1° rotation costs 0.91 / 3.20 dB on Bonsai / Room. Photometric repair and full BA camera transfer both failed their contracts.
  • Rotation-only transfer. Take BA's rotation, keep each input camera centre, hold cameras fixed during training.
